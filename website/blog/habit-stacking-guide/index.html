Habit Stacking Guide

Habit stacking is the practice of attaching a new habit to one you already do reliably. It works because the first action becomes the cue for the next, which reduces the need to remember from scratch each time.

What habit stacking really is

A stack usually sounds like this: after I make coffee, I will write three lines in my journal. After I brush my teeth, I will stretch for one minute. The power comes from borrowing stability from an existing behaviour.

This connects closely to the cue-routine-reward model. The old habit provides the cue. The new behaviour becomes the routine. The sense of completion or calm becomes the reward.

How to chain habits correctly

The best stacks are specific, small and physical. Choose an anchor that already happens at a predictable time and place. Then attach a behaviour that can realistically happen in that same context. If the jump is too large, the chain breaks.

For example, after opening your laptop you might review one priority, but you probably should not attach a 45-minute workout to boiling the kettle. Stacks work best when the follow-up action feels like a natural next move.

Common mistakes

The biggest mistake is stacking too much at once. Another is choosing an anchor that is not actually stable. If your anchor habit varies wildly by time or location, the new habit has no reliable base.
